# Gaussian-mixture model of the linkage statistics

`gmm_chain.py` is the model; `example.py` fits it and uses it. The scripts in `../analysis/` that produce the
tables of the paper contain the same model with the random draws fixed.

## 1. What the model is for

A simulation of a chain of 6, 12 or 18 residues gives the conformations of that chain. The unperturbed dimensions
of amylose are those of a long chain. The model learns how one glycosidic linkage behaves in the simulations and
generates as many linkages as needed, so that chains of any length can be built and their characteristic ratio
computed. Because the model separates the linkages into kinked (anti-ψ) and *syn* ones, it also allows chains
that no simulation contains: without kinks, or with the kinks of one force field and the *syn* linkages of the
other.

## 2. What is known about one linkage

Each glucose residue carries a frame built from three of its atoms: **x** along C1→C4, **y** towards O5 in the
plane C1–C4–O5, **z** = **x** × **y**. For the linkage between residues *i* and *i*+1 the data hold

- the rotation $R_i$ that converts the components of a vector in the frame of residue *i*+1 into its components
  in the frame of residue *i* (a 3 × 3 matrix);
- the virtual bond $v_{i+1}$, from the glycosidic oxygen O4(*i*) to O4(*i*+1), which crosses residue *i*+1,
  written in the frame of that residue (3 numbers, nm);
- the torsion $\psi_i$ = C1(*i*+1)–O4(*i*)–C4(*i*)–C5(*i*).

A linkage is a kink (anti-ψ) when $|\psi + 117.8^\circ| > 90^\circ$, and *syn* otherwise.

## 3. The descriptor

One linkage is described by eleven numbers,

$$\mathbf x = \big(\ \text{first column of } R_i,\ \ \text{second column of } R_i,\ \ v_{i+1},\ \ \cos\psi_i,\ \ \sin\psi_i\ \big).$$

The third column of a rotation is the vector product of the first two, so it carries no further information.
$\psi$ enters through its cosine and sine so that $-180^\circ$ and $+180^\circ$ are the same point.

## 4. The fit

The joint distribution of $\mathbf x$ is written as a sum of $K$ Gaussians,

$$P(\mathbf x)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\pi_k\,\mathcal N(\mathbf x\,|\,\boldsymbol\mu_k,\boldsymbol\Sigma_k),$$

with weights $\pi_k$, means $\boldsymbol\mu_k$ and full covariance matrices $\boldsymbol\Sigma_k$, fitted by
expectation–maximisation (scikit-learn) to the standardized descriptors of at most 120 000 linkage observations
drawn at random. $K = 12$: the log-likelihood of a replica left out of the fit and the resulting $C_\infty$ stop
changing at that number (`../analysis/chain_model.py`, key `components_test`).

A component is a **kink component** when the $\psi$ of its mean is anti-ψ, and a ***syn* component** otherwise.
The sum of the weights of the kink components is the kink weight of the model; it reproduces the anti-ψ population
of the simulations.

## 5. Generating linkages and chains

A linkage is generated by choosing a component with probability $\pi_k$ and drawing $\mathbf x$ from its
Gaussian. The two generated columns are made orthonormal (Gram–Schmidt) and the third is their vector product,
which restores a proper rotation. A chain of $N$ linkages is built by applying the generated rotations and bonds
one after another: with $F_1 = I$,

$$F_{i+1}=F_i\,R_i,\qquad \vec r_{i+1}=\vec r_i+F_{i+1}\,v_{i+1},$$

where $\vec r_i$ is the position of the glycosidic oxygen O4(*i*). Successive linkages are drawn independently.

## 6. The characteristic ratio

From $n$ generated linkages (400 000 in the paper), with $\bar R=\langle R_i\rangle$, $v=\langle v_i\rangle$,
$w=\langle R_i v_{i+1}\rangle$ and $l^2=\langle|v|^2\rangle$,

$$C_\infty = 1+\frac{2}{l^2}\,v^{\mathsf T}(I-\bar R)^{-1}w ,\qquad
C_N = 1+\frac{2}{N l^2}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(N-k)\,v^{\mathsf T}\bar R^{\,k-1}w .$$

The same $C_N$ is obtained, within sampling error, from the end-to-end distances of chains built as in
section 5.

## 7. Changing the kink weight

Because kink and *syn* components are separate, linkages can be generated with any fraction $p$ of kinks:
$(1-p)\,n$ linkages from the *syn* components and $p\,n$ from the kink components.

- $p = 0$: chains without kinks.
- *syn* components of one force field with the kink components and kink weight of the other.
- $p$ varied until $C_\infty$ equals an experimental value; the corresponding kink free energy is
  $\Delta G = RT\ln[(1-p)/p]$.

These are the calculations of `../analysis/gmm_controls.py` (Tables 8 and 10 of the paper).

## 8. How to use it

```python
from gmm_chain import load_linkages, LinkageModel

R, v, phipsi = load_linkages(["SC6_r2", "SC6_r4", "SC6_r6", "SC6_r8", "SC6_r10"])   # CHARMM36 hexamer
model = LinkageModel(R, v, phipsi)             # fit: 12 components

model.kink_weight                              # weight of the kink components
model.numbers()                                # {'Cinf': ..., 'bond_nm': ..., 'kink_fraction': ...}
model.numbers(kink_weight=0.0)                 # chains without kinks
model.linkages(1000)                           # R, v, psi of 1000 generated linkages
model.chains(500, 99)                          # 500 chains of 100 residues: positions of the glycosidic oxygens
```

`python example.py` runs the two force fields and prints, for each, the kink weight, $C_\infty$ with and without
kinks, and the size of generated 18-residue chains (about two minutes).

## 9. What the model does not contain

Successive linkages are generated independently; the test of that assumption on the simulations is in
`../analysis/independence_test.py`. The model reproduces the statistics of the force field it was fitted to; it
does not correct them.
